# Earthquake Preparedness Guide

## Overview
Earthquakes release energy stored in the earth's crust, producing ground shaking that can damage structures, utilities, transportation corridors, and communications.

## Fault and shaking basics
- **Strike-slip faults:** horizontal movement along a fault line
- **Normal faults:** one block drops relative to another
- **Reverse or thrust faults:** compression pushes one block over another

Earthquake severity is commonly described by both magnitude and observed shaking intensity. A moderate event can still cause serious local damage depending on depth, soil conditions, and building quality.

## Structural risks
Common earthquake hazards include:
- falling furniture and unsecured shelving
- broken glass and interior debris
- masonry failure
- gas leaks and electrical faults
- water line breaks and sanitation disruption

## Immediate response
During shaking:
- drop to the ground
- take cover under sturdy furniture or beside a solid interior barrier
- hold on until shaking stops
- stay away from windows and tall unsecured objects

After shaking:
- check for injuries
- shut off utilities if there is clear evidence of damage or leakage
- expect aftershocks
- avoid damaged buildings until they are assessed

## Supply priorities
Earthquake preparedness should emphasize:
- water stored in stable containers
- trauma supplies and first aid capability
- flashlights and headlamps
- shoes and gloves near beds
- backup communications
- shelf-stable food that does not require much cooking
- tools to shut off gas or water if needed

## Planning guidance
Secure heavy furniture, water heaters, and shelving before an event. Store critical items in multiple locations so one damaged room does not eliminate access to essentials. Build household plans around reunification, utility shutoff decisions, and short-notice self-sufficiency.
