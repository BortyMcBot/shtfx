# Civil Unrest Preparedness Guide

## Overview
Civil unrest can range from isolated disturbances to widespread protests, opportunistic crime, transportation disruption, or temporary interruption of normal services.

## 2026 refresh
- Ready.gov's current disaster guidance still reinforces the boring but critical basics: know your trusted alert sources, decide in advance where you would go if you had to leave, and keep a go-bag ready instead of improvising under stress.
- For unrest scenarios, route flexibility matters more than a single perfect plan. Households should know multiple directions out, alternate meeting points, and clear triggers for when staying put is safer than moving.
- Situational awareness should combine official local alerts, transportation status, and direct observation. Rumor-driven panic can create risk even when the core incident is limited.

## Situational awareness priorities
- confirm reports from multiple sources before acting
- monitor official alerts, traffic conditions, and service interruptions
- distinguish nearby events from broader rumor-driven panic
- avoid unnecessary travel into unstable areas

## Shelter-in-place vs evacuation framework
Choose shelter-in-place when:
- home remains structurally safe
- unrest is localized and movement increases exposure
- supplies and communications are adequate for a short duration
- officials have not identified a safer evacuation path

Choose evacuation when:
- violence is moving toward the area
- access to medical care or essential services is collapsing
- the home cannot be secured or safely occupied
- staying creates a higher probability of being trapped
- authorities direct movement away from the area

## Communication discipline
- keep messages short, factual, and time-stamped
- avoid broadcasting household plans publicly
- use agreed rally points and check-in rules
- preserve battery and bandwidth for essential updates
- tell household members which information sources are trusted before a crisis starts

## Planning guidance
Civil unrest planning benefits from low-profile behavior, flexible routing, and clear household decision triggers. Focus on staying informed, avoiding exposure, and reducing the amount of attention your household draws during uncertain conditions.
