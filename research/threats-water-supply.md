# Water Supply Disruption Preparedness Guide

## Overview
Water service can fail because of contamination events, power loss, line breaks, treatment failures, drought restrictions, frozen infrastructure, or upstream supply interruption.

## Common disruption scenarios
- boil-water advisories after contamination risk
- pressure loss from line failures or pumping problems
- service interruption after storms, outages, or infrastructure damage
- reduced availability during drought or emergency rationing

## Storage math
A common minimum planning baseline is one gallon per person per day for drinking and basic sanitation. Real needs can be higher due to heat, illness, pets, cooking needs, and hygiene requirements. Storage plans should account for both duration and replenishment options.

## Purification methods
- boiling when fuel and time are available
- gravity or pump filtration for particulates and some biological risks
- chemical treatment for biological contamination when used correctly
- commercially sealed water as a low-maintenance reserve

## Contamination risks
Not all water problems are solved the same way. Some treatments handle biological risks but not chemical contamination. Households should understand the limits of their filters and avoid assumptions when source quality is uncertain.

## Planning guidance
Store water in multiple container sizes rather than one giant reserve. Label fill dates, rotate supplies, and keep a simple rationing plan. Pair stored water with containers, purification methods, and sanitation supplies so the household can manage both drinking and hygiene during an extended disruption.
