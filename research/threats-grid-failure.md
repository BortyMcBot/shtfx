# Grid Failure Preparedness Guide

## Overview
Grid failure can result from storms, equipment failure, fuel shortages, cyber incidents, wildfire shutoffs, heat stress, or cascading utility faults. Duration may range from a few hours to many days.

## What outages affect first
Power outages often disrupt more than lighting:
- refrigeration and freezer storage
- water pumping and pressure systems
- internet and cellular infrastructure
- heating and cooling
- fuel station operations
- payment systems and ATMs

## Duration planning
Plan in layers:
- **24 hours:** lighting, device charging, food protection
- **72 hours:** refrigeration strategy, water access, communications discipline
- **1 week or more:** fuel rotation, sanitation, cooling/heating, workload management

## Power backup options
- portable battery banks for phones and small electronics
- solar generators or battery stations for quiet indoor-safe backup
- fuel generators for larger loads when operated safely outdoors
- vehicle charging as a short-duration fallback

## Food and water implications
Without power, refrigerated food becomes time-sensitive. Water access may also degrade if pumps, treatment systems, or building pressure systems depend on electricity. Keep thermometers, coolers, stored water, and no-cook food available.

## Communication fallbacks
- preserve battery by reducing screen time and background apps
- establish scheduled check-ins instead of constant updates
- keep paper contact lists and offline maps
- use radios when available

## Planning guidance
List critical electrical loads by priority rather than trying to power everything. Identify what must run, for how long, and with what power source. Practice a short outage drill so the household learns where lighting, charging gear, and manual alternatives are kept.
